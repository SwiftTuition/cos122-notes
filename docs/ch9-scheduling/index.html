Uniprocessor Scheduling

Chapter 9 of COS 122 Operating Systems.

How an operating system decides which process runs on a single processor: the four kinds of scheduling, the criteria a policy is judged by, and the short-term policies FCFS, round robin, SPN, SRT, HRRN, Feedback and Priority. The exam questions draw time-scale diagrams, compute turnaround and waiting times, name a policy from a schedule and work out HRRN ratios.

Rules used in every answer

  • A process that arrives at time t is ready for the decision made at time t.
  • Round robin: when a quantum ends at the same time as a new process arrives, the new process joins the queue first.
  • SRT: a newcomer preempts only if it needs strictly less time than the running process has left.
  • Priority: a lower number means a higher priority.
  • Feedback: preemption only when a quantum ends; a process alone keeps its queue; the lowest-queue rule is stated in each question.
  • Waiting time = turnaround − service time.

Assumed

When SPN, SRT, HRRN or Priority must choose between two processes with equal values, the earlier arrival goes first. No question in the course material depends on this, and the questions on these pages avoid such ties except where this is said.
