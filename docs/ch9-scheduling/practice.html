Feedback rules for this question

  • Quantum: q = 1 in every queue.
  • A new process joins the back of RQ0 (RQ means ready queue).
  • The scheduler runs the front process of the highest queue that is not empty. Each queue is FCFS.
  • Preemption happens only when a quantum ends. A process that arrives in the middle of a quantum waits.
  • If a process uses its whole quantum and another process is waiting, it moves down one queue. If nothing else is waiting, it stays and starts a new quantum.
  • There is no lowest queue: the number of queues is not limited, so a process that is preempted always moves down one more queue (the textbook rule).

Task 16: Feedback q = 1 compared with q = 2i

Exam-typical (level 2 of 3) Topic: Feedback.

What is the difference between Feedback with quantum q = 1 and Feedback with q = 2i? Both are variations of the Feedback policy. Is one better than the other?

Write your answer before you open the solution.

Both use the same queues: new processes enter RQ0 and drop one queue after each preemption. With q = 1 every queue gets a quantum of 1. With q = 2i the quantum doubles at each lower queue (RQ0: 1, RQ1: 2, RQ2: 4, ...).

With q = 1 a long process moves down to a low queue. There it gets one unit at a time. So its turnaround keeps taking longer. It can starve while new processes keep arriving. With q = 2i such a process gets a longer run each time it is chosen. So it is less likely to starve.

Is one better? Look at the numbers for a workload. On the textbook workload q = 1 is better on both measures: mean Tr 10 for q = 1 against 10.60 for q = 2i, and mean Tr/Ts 2.29 against 2.63. The benefit of q = 2i appears when new processes keep arriving and a long process would otherwise starve.
