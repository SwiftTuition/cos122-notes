Example 6: Feedback with q = 1

Exam-typical (level 2 of 3) Workload: the table at the top of the page.

Feedback rules for this question

  • Quantum: q = 1 in every queue.
  • A new process joins the back of RQ0 (RQ means ready queue).
  • The scheduler runs the front process of the highest queue that is not empty. Each queue is FCFS.
  • Preemption happens only when a quantum ends. A process that arrives in the middle of a quantum waits.
  • If a process uses its whole quantum and another process is waiting, it moves down one queue. If nothing else is waiting, it stays and starts a new quantum.
  • There is no lowest queue: the number of queues is not limited, so a process that is preempted always moves down one more queue (the textbook rule).

  • Quantum: q = 2i: RQ0 has q = 1, RQ1 has q = 2, RQ2 has q = 4, RQ3 has q = 8, and so on.

Example 11: Naming the policy from a schedule

Exam-typical (level 2 of 3) Same shape as a past exam question.

A schedule for the workload at the top of the page is given. Name a policy that produces it.

Checks, in this order

  1. Is a process interrupted before it finishes? If not, the policy is non-preemptive: FCFS, SPN, HRRN or non-preemptive priority. Compare the order at each free moment with arrival order (FCFS), shortest service (SPN) or highest ratio (HRRN).
  2. If processes are interrupted, check where: only at arrivals (SRT, preemptive priority) or at regular quantum ends (RR, Feedback).
  3. Confirm with one decision: at that time, does the chosen process match the rule?

Here P1 runs 0 to 2, P2 runs 2 to 3, then P1 continues: P1 was interrupted at 2, exactly when P2 arrived. P2 needs 1 and P1 had 4 left. Next, P4 (arrival 5) does not interrupt P1 at 5, because P4 needs 4 and P1 has 2 left. Interruptions happen only at arrivals, and the process with the least time left always runs.

It is not preemptive priority, even though that policy also interrupts only at arrivals. At time 5, P4 (priority 2) has a higher priority than P1 (priority 3), so preemptive priority would run P4. Here P1 keeps running, which fits the time left, not the priority.
